## Why this route exists

Retention is cheaper than acquisition, but a churn rate does not tell support
who to call. This route ranks households, shows the evidence behind each score,
and makes the next action available in the same place — answering **who, why,
and what now**.

## What I chose — and what I left out

| Option                          | Why I rejected it                     | What I chose instead                             |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| A churn-rate trend chart        | Shows the outcome, not who to call    | Risk table first, trends second                  |
| A single risk-score column      | A number nobody can defend in a call  | Score, band, signals, and a household drawer     |
| One global list with no filters | Too broad for a support shift         | Band, area, and status filters                   |
| An ML churn probability         | No labelled outcomes; hard to explain | A transparent 0–100 heuristic with named weights |

## The data behind it

The seed puts about **13%** of households in a risk cohort: a 60–120 day window
with two to four complaints and late or failed payments across that window.
About **60%** cancel one to three months after it. Everyone else gets Poisson
complaints and an 8% base churn rate.

Risk is computed by one shared function, not hard-coded in the seed. The seed
calls `recomputeAllRiskScores()` after inserting complaints, payments, and
tickets, so seeded scores and the in-app button use the same code path.

The weights are readable:

- **Complaints (max 45):** 9 per complaint in 90 days, plus 5 per high-severity
  complaint and 5 per unresolved complaint.
- **Payments (max 45):** 7 per late payment and 11 per failed payment over six
  months.
- **SLA (max 16):** 4 per missed-SLA ticket in 12 months.
- **Tenure (max 8):** 8 points under 90 days, 5 under 180, 2 under a year.

The result is clamped to 0–100 and banded at 34 and 67.

## Interactions

- **Sort, filter, page.** Every change is a tRPC input, not client-side slicing;
  filters reset to page 1 and rows dim until the next page resolves.
- **Open a household.** Only Zustand state changes; the drawer fetches its own
  profile and leaves the table untouched.
- **Add or resolve a complaint.** Toast, then invalidate the affected profile
  and list. Adding also refreshes the trend; both refresh the risk table.
- **Recompute risk.** Confirmation, toast with the household count, then
  invalidate table, distribution, KPIs, and profile. No optimistic scoring —
  showing a new rank before the server re-scores would mislead.

## What to notice as a reviewer

1. Filter to **High** risk and open a household. The drawer shows the exact
   complaints, payments, and SLA-missed tickets behind the score.
2. Read
   <SourceLink
     path="src/db/queries/risk.ts"
     symbol="computeHouseholdRisk"

   — one function with named weights.
3. Resolve a complaint, then click **Recompute risk**. The table re-sorts and
   the donut re-reads the rewritten scores.

## Trade-offs & next

- The score is a ranking heuristic, not a calibrated probability. Weights are
  hand-tuned and global, with no per-plan or per-area calibration and no outcome
  tracking to fit them against real cancellations.
- Adding or resolving a complaint does not automatically re-score, so the donut
  can lag the evidence.
- No suggested next action: the page says who to call and why, not whether to
  offer a discount, a filter change, or an apology call.
- Table state is local, so filters and sort reset on navigation; the drawer caps
  history at 50 tickets, 50 complaints, and 36 payments.
