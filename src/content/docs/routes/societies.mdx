## Why this route exists

Growth depends on **where** the next society is, not just how many households
exist. The team needs to compare areas on active households, penetration against
flats covered, open tickets, and average TDS. The page answers **where should we
expand, and which society is underperforming?**

## What I chose — and what I left out

| Option                         | Why I rejected it                      | What I chose instead                          |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| A map of Bangalore             | Nice visual, weak comparison           | Stacked growth, penetration bars, and a table |
| One growth line                | Hides the area mix                     | Stacked area by area                          |
| A penetration donut            | Part-to-whole does not fit eight areas | Horizontal bar, sorted                        |
| Cards per society              | 50 cards is not scannable              | Server-paged table plus a drawer              |
| A separate society detail page | Slower context switching               | Drawer with plan mix and top risk             |

### Society table

### Society drawer

A row opens a right-side drawer with the society facts: area, name, onboarded
date, flats covered, penetration, open tickets, 30-day average TDS, and active
households. Below that, the plan mix (starter, standard, premium as a share of
active subscriptions) and the five highest-risk active households, each with
tenure and risk band. "Open in Customers" hands off to the risk flow.

The drawer owns a separate, non-suspense query, so opening a row never refetches
or re-sorts the table, and the table state survives. It keeps the last selected
id through the close animation and offers a Retry on a failed fetch.

## The data behind it

- **50 societies across 8 areas.** The area allocation is weighted toward
  high-infrastructure neighbourhoods; Whitefield (14 years), Electronic City
  (12), and Sarjapur Road (10) carry the largest footprints.
- **Onboarding waves over the first 14 months.** The wave weights are
  front-loaded: six societies in each of the first two months, a 4–5/month
  plateau through month seven, then a taper to one by month 14.
- **80–400 flats per society.** Each society gets 12–34 households. About 78%
  join within 3–45 days of onboarding; the rest trickle in three to nine months
  later. A 7% recent-joiner slice keeps the growth curve alive inside existing
  societies.
- **Penetration** is active households ÷ flats covered by onboarded societies,
  not every flat in Bangalore. Flats covered only counts societies ProWater has
  already onboarded.
- **Risk** in the drawer is the stored score written by the same
  `recomputeAllRiskScores()` path used on `/customers`, so the top five match
  the risk table rather than a separate society-only heuristic.

## Interactions

- **Search, filter, sort, page.** Every change is a tRPC input, not client-side
  slicing. Search is debounced into one request; filters reset to page 1; the
  current rows dim while the next result loads because a deferred copy of the
  state drives the table.
- **Open a society.** Clicking a row only updates the Zustand UI store. The
  drawer fetches its own profile, so the table does not refetch and its page,
  sort, filter, and search state are preserved.
- **Close the drawer.** The last id is snapshotted so the content does not blank
  during the exit animation.
- **Read-only.** There are no mutations on this route. The next action is the
  link to `/customers` for the high-risk households.

## What to notice as a reviewer

1. Open `growthSeriesKeys` and the growth card together. The keys come from the
   first data point, not a hardcoded array; the chart and legend both map over
   that list, so a new area cannot be dropped silently. With the current eight
   seeded areas, "Other" stays at zero.
2. Sort the society table by penetration, then hover the top area bar. The
   tooltip adds infrastructure age and tickets per 100 households — the "why"
   behind the ranking.
3. Open a society with high penetration and few open tickets. The drawer should
   show a healthy plan mix and few high-risk households; contrast it with an
   older-infrastructure area.

## Trade-offs & next

- Penetration uses flats covered by onboarded societies, not all flats in an
  area, so a new area with no societies shows as zero rather than untapped.
- No map view. The table and bars compare better, but a map would help field
  planning and route density.
- No lease or contract data per society, so the page cannot distinguish a
  long-term partner from a month-to-month arrangement.
- No expansion scoring model. The page gives the inputs — penetration, service
  load, infrastructure age — but not a ranked recommendation.
- The drawer is read-only. Creating a ticket or changing a plan still happens on
  the relevant route.
