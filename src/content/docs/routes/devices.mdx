## Why this route exists

The dispenser fleet is the physical edge of the business. When a device goes
offline, its membrane degrades, or purity slips, the customer notices before the
dashboard does. Ops needs one answer each morning: **which devices are offline,
degrading, or producing bad water?**

With roughly a thousand dispensers, a raw inventory is not an answer. The route
turns the fleet into a short attention list and keeps evidence one click away.

## What I chose - and what I left out

| Option                  | Why I rejected it                                    | What I chose instead                       |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| A table of every device | Too much for a monitoring overview                   | Aggregate charts plus an anomaly feed      |
| One chart per metric    | No prioritisation; every device looks equally urgent | Anomaly mix, fleet trend, and online gauge |
| A map of devices        | Interesting, but not the first operational decision  | Severity-sorted anomaly feed               |
| Real-time streaming     | Overkill for a simulated demo                        | Daily aggregates plus latest readings      |

## What's on screen

Live route: `/devices`. The device drawer is the detail state. The page is
`force-dynamic`, and `batchPrefetchDevicesOverview()` warms the fleet stats, the
30-day telemetry trend, and the anomaly feed. Device readings are excluded on
purpose: the drawer owns its windowed query and fetches on open.

### KPI row

Four tiles from `getFleetStats`:

- **Online** - share of active-household dispensers with `is_online = true`.
- **Avg purity (7d)** - mean purity across every reading in the last seven days.
- **Needs service** - dispensers whose filter is older than 75 days, latest TDS
  is above 450 ppm, or latest purity is below 85%.
- **Offline > 7d** - dispensers flagged offline **or** with no reading in the
  last seven days.

The percentage reads the stored `is_online` flag; the offline count also catches
devices that are nominally online but have gone quiet.

### Anomaly mix

A donut of the anomaly feed, bucketed by each device's **most urgent signal**:
offline or silent, high TDS, then low purity. Each device counts once, in
priority order, so the percentages describe devices rather than raw alarm
counts. The centre reports the number of flagged devices in the feed.

Because the feed is capped at the worst 100 devices, the donut describes that

## The data behind it

Each household gets one dispenser, installed about two to seven days after
joining. Starter plans use the household's own device; standard and premium get
the matching model. About 96% are online; an offline device stops reporting nine
to 75 days ago.

Telemetry follows the filter cycle:

- Cycles last **60–75 days**. TDS starts at 105–140 ppm and climbs to 385–470
  ppm, or 480–560 in high-infrastructure areas, on a convex `progress^1.12`
  curve.
- Purity tracks TDS: about 97.5% fresh, about 84% at the ceiling. Hardness
  follows the same progress with a monsoon multiplier.
- Readings land every two to three days, 06:00–22:00, with noise; pH stays
  6.8–7.4.
- TDS crossing 500 ppm before the scheduled end emits a repair (plus a 4%
  hardware-fault path); the scheduled end emits a filter change.

Anomaly detection is deterministic. It takes each dispenser's latest reading
from the last 30 days and flags offline, no reading for over seven days, TDS
above 450 ppm, or purity below 85%. Severity is high when TDS is above 500,
purity below 84%, or the latest reading is over 14 days old; otherwise medium.
Results sort by severity and TDS, then cap at 100.

## Interactions

- **Switch the trend window.** Tabs update local state; a deferred copy drives
  the chart, so the current curve dims instead of flashing a skeleton.
- **Open a device.** "View device" writes to a small Zustand store; the drawer
  fetches `getReadings` for the chosen window.
- **Create a ticket.** The action calls `serviceOps.createTicket`, choosing a
  filter change when TDS is above 450 or purity is below 85, otherwise a repair.
  A toast reports the ticket, and the hook invalidates anomalies, fleet, board,
  SLA, and area demand.
- **No telemetry writes.** The drawer is read-only; there is no "mark fixed"
  action. The service ticket is the write.

## What to notice as a reviewer

1. The anomaly feed is urgency-sorted, not id-sorted. The top row is the
   highest-TDS device in the top severity group; open it and compare the chart
   with the stated reason.
2. Open a drawer and switch from 30 to 90 days. The sawtooth is the filter
   cycle: TDS climbs, then resets after a change. Hover the first reading after
   the reset date for the **Filter reset** tooltip row.

## Trade-offs & next

- Anomaly rules are heuristic and global: own-device and premium installations
  share the same TDS and purity cut-offs.
- There is no real-time ingestion, alerting, or paging; the page is a snapshot
  on request.
- The anomaly mix describes only the capped feed, not the whole fleet.
- The trend is fleet-wide and unsegmented - no area, plan, or device-model
  filter - and the drawer loads on demand.
- Connectivity is seeded. The status dot reports the stored flag; the table's
  flag also treats a silent device as offline, so the two can disagree.
