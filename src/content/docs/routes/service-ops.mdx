## Why this route exists

Service ops has two jobs: **triage today's work** and **prevent tomorrow's
failures**. The page pairs a kanban with a predictive queue, then exposes
capacity: area demand, SLA performance, and technician load. The decision is
"who needs a visit next, and who is going to do it?"

## What I chose — and what I left out

| Option                     | Why I rejected it                       | What I chose instead             |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Status dropdown in a table | Slower to triage; no sense of flow      | Four-column drag-and-drop kanban |
| Calendar view              | Good for scheduling, not for triage     | Kanban plus predictive table     |
| Only the predictive list   | No way to manage current work           | Kanban plus predictive table     |
| A map of tickets           | Interesting, but not the first decision | Area demand bars plus a table    |

The header opens a create dialog; dropping on Assigned without a technician

behind the shared `confirm()` dialog.

## The data behind it

- **Tickets are causal.** `simulateDispenserLife` builds 60–75 day filter
  cycles. TDS climbs from 105–140 ppm toward 385–470 ppm, or 480–560 in
  high-infra areas. Crossing 500 ppm before the scheduled change emits a
  `repair`; the scheduled end emits a `filter_change`.
- **Telemetry sets the curve.** Readings land every 2–3 days with noise and a
  monsoon hardness bump; the predictive query keeps the latest six per
  dispenser.
- **Extra visits.** About 70% of households get an installation, and each
  completed cycle has a 6% chance of an inspection.
- **Backlog is seeded.** Six percent of tickets older than 14 days stay open,
  feeding the overdue KPI.
- **SLA targets.** Repair 48h, filter change 72h, inspection 96h, installation
  120h. Seed resolution times are wider, so some tickets miss.
- **Capacity is weighted.** Whitefield has a technician at weight 5; Electronic
  City and Sarjapur Road have one at 4, with a long tail at 1. That is the
  workload chart's shape.
- **Area stress is structural.** Infrastructure ages are 14 years in Whitefield,
  12 in Electronic City, and 10 in Sarjapur Road, against 3–6 elsewhere.

The predictive heuristic is explicit: a 30-day window, the latest six readings,
an ordinary-least-squares slope, and two triggers — `slope ≥ 3.5 ppm/day` with
`latest TDS ≥ 250`, or `filter age ≥ 70 days`. Candidates are scored with
`latestTds + slopePpmPerDay × 10 + filterAgeDays × 2` and capped at 25.

<Callout type="info" title="A heuristic, not a model">
  This is a batch query with named constants, not ML. Every row explains itself,
  so a dispatcher can defend the visit. See the [method
  note](/docs/ideas/predictive-service-demand).
</Callout>

## Interactions

- **Drag a card.** `useUpdateTicketStatus` snapshots the board cache, patches
  the status (and `resolvedDate` when resolving), and rolls back on error. It
  invalidates board, SLA, and workload; toasts confirm or explain. The hook:

  <SourceLink
    path="src/features/service-ops/hooks/use-update-ticket-status.ts"
    label="use-update-ticket-status.ts"
  />

- **Resolve.** The query stamps today's `resolvedDate` and computes `slaMet`
  against the type's target; reopening clears both.
- **Create.** The dialog invalidates board, SLA, and area demand. There is no
  optimistic insert because the ticket needs a server id.
- **Assign.** `useAssignTicket` patches the board with the technician id and
  name, then invalidates board and workload. Dropping on Assigned without a
  technician opens the dialog instead of mutating.
- **Delete.** `useRemoveTicket` removes the card optimistically after
  confirmation, then invalidates board, SLA, and workload.

The board is shared by every demo visitor: there is no auth in this project, so
one visitor's drag changes what the next visitor sees. That is a demo decision,
not a production one.

## What to notice as a reviewer

1. Drag a card, watch the optimistic move, then refresh. The persisted status
   should match. The rollback path is in `useUpdateTicketStatus`.
2. Compare the predictive table's top rows with the area demand chart. The
   high-stress areas — Whitefield, Electronic City, Sarjapur Road — should line
   up, because both read the same simulated infrastructure stress.
3. Read `getPredictive`: the `row_number()` window keeps the latest six readings
   per dispenser, `linearSlope` fits the line in TypeScript, and the score is a
   plain weighted sum. There is no hidden model.

## Trade-offs & next

- The kanban is a triage surface, not a dispatch optimiser: no travel time,
  skills, or spare-parts routing.
- There is no calendar capacity planning or technician shift model.
- The predictive queue is batch, not streaming; it recomputes on page load.
- The resolved column stops at 60 tickets from the last 30 days; there is no
  full ticket archive.
- Mutations are global. Production needs auth, assignment rules, and an audit
  trail.
