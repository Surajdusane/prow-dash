## Why this route exists

The first question an ops lead asks on a Monday morning is: _is the business
healthy this month?_ The overview has to answer that in under a minute:
households, revenue, churn, open service load, and fleet water quality. It is
also the entry point to every deeper route.

The page is not a data catalogue. It is a hierarchy: six numbers first, four
supporting visuals second, one cross-area table last. Everything else belongs on
a dedicated route.

## What I chose - and what I left out

| Option                   | Why I rejected it                                 | What I chose instead                       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| A wall of every metric   | No hierarchy; nothing stands out                  | Six KPIs plus four supporting visuals      |
| Only charts, no numbers  | Slow to scan; the answer is usually in the number | KPI row first, then trends                 |
| A single revenue chart   | Misses service and fleet health                   | Revenue, growth, plan mix, and area health |
| A map of Bangalore       | Interesting, but not the first decision           | Area health table with TDS and SLA         |
| A configurable dashboard | Role configuration is a product, not a demo       | Fixed composition, optimized for one job   |

## What's on screen

The page is a server component (`force-dynamic`) that calls
`batchPrefetchOverview()`, wraps the tree in `HydrateClient`, and splits each
section into its own `Suspense` boundary. The page component does not fetch
directly; each card reads the same query key through a suspense hook.

<Callout type="info" title="The churn denominator matters">
  Churn is not `cancelled ÷ active today`. The denominator is households that
  had joined by the 90-day cutoff and had not cancelled by it - the active base
  at the start of the window. The numerator is any household whose cancellation
  falls after the cutoff. That keeps the percentage comparable across a growing
  book, though a household that joins and cancels inside the window can land in
  the numerator but not the denominator.
</Callout>

## The data behind it

- **Growth waves.** Societies onboard across months 1–14 of the 24-month window;
  78% of households join within 45 days of their society onboarding, and a 7%
  slice joins in the last 90 days. That produces the cumulative growth curve and
  the "new this month" KPI. Seed sources:

  - <SourceLink
      path="src/db/seed/generators/societies.ts"
      label="societies.ts"
    />
  - <SourceLink
      path="src/db/seed/generators/households.ts"
      label="households.ts"
    />

- **Plan-mix shift.** Starter share falls from 45% to 12% over the window while
  premium rises from 8% to 35%, so the donut reflects a business moving upmarket
  rather than a static random split.
- **Churn.** A ~13% risk cohort carries the bad windows; ~60% of that cohort
  cancels 1–3 months later, on top of an 8% base churn rate.
- **Billing.** Payments are generated per active subscription-month. The current
  month is weighted 58% paid / 5% late / 4% failed / 33% pending; older months
  are 88% paid / 8% late / 4% failed. Risk-cohort months are 20% paid / 50% late
  / 30% failed. That is why billed and collected diverge without a random walk.
- **Area health.** `areas.infrastructure_age_years` is the lever: Whitefield (14
  years), Electronic City (12), and Sarjapur Road (10) degrade filters faster,
  which shows up in TDS and ticket volume.

Windows are explicit: 18 months for billing, 24 months for growth, 30 days for
TDS, 90 days for SLA. The page is `force-dynamic`, so those windows end today,
not at build time.

## Interactions

There are no mutations on the overview. It is a read-only summary, and the cards
are not links themselves; the dashboard sidebar is the navigation surface to the
deeper routes. Each section still maps to the route that owns its decision:
revenue to `/revenue`, service load to `/service-ops`, churn to `/customers`,
and fleet purity to `/devices`.

Each section has its own `Suspense` fallback (`KpiRowSkeleton`,
`ChartCardSkeleton`, `TableCardSkeleton`) and the route shares one error
boundary with a retry button. A cold database shows the retry state, not a blank
page.

## What to notice as a reviewer

1. The churn KPI uses the active base at the start of the 90-day window, not
   today's active count. Read `getOverviewKpis` and check the four `filter`
   clauses in the household aggregate.
2. Area health is five grouped queries in one `Promise.all`, then a TypeScript
   merge by `areaId` - not five sequential queries and not an N+1 loop.
3. The page has no client-side data fetching of its own: `batchPrefetchOverview`
   warms the cache and every card reads the same query key through
   `useSuspenseQuery`.

## Trade-offs & next

- The KPI row is fixed. A configurable dashboard would suit different roles
  better, but it would also make the demo's hierarchy disappear.
- Billing stops at 18 months to keep the chart readable. A longer window would
  compress the recent gap that matters.
- There is no previous-period comparison. Delta indicators would make the KPIs
  more decision-ready than absolute numbers.
- Area health is a table. A map would be a nice addition, but the table answers
  the comparison question faster.
- `getHouseholdGrowth` shapes 24 windows in TypeScript. At ~1,200 households
  that is cheap and readable; at a larger book it should move into a SQL
  aggregate.
