## Why this route exists

Revenue is not one number. An ops lead needs five answers at once: is MRR
rising, are customers moving up-plan or down-plan, how much is late or failed,
do newer cohorts stick, and which overdue payments can be recovered today. The
page answers **where revenue is leaking, and which cohort is weak**.

## What I chose — and what I left out

| Option                  | Why I rejected it                                 | What I chose instead                                     |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| One revenue chart       | Does not show mix or leakage                      | Five surfaces: MRR, plan mix, outcomes, cohorts, overdue |
| Revenue by area         | Interesting, but not the primary revenue decision | MRR and plan mix first                                   |
| A single "churn" number | Hides cohort differences                          | Cohort retention matrix                                  |
| A payments table only   | No trend or mix                                   | Stacked payment outcomes plus an overdue action table    |
| ARR / forecast          | Not enough history to make a forecast honest      | MRR only, clearly labelled as a run-rate                 |

## What's on screen

Live route: `/revenue`. The page is `force-dynamic`; `batchPrefetchRevenue()`
warms the KPI row, four charts, and the first overdue page in one batch. Each
card has its own Suspense boundary and a matching skeleton.

### KPI row

Five tiles from `getRevenueKpis`:

- **MRR** — the sum of active plan prices. It is a run-rate, not cash collected.
- **ARPU** — MRR ÷ active subscriptions, rounded to whole rupees.
- **Active subs** — subscriptions billing today.
- **Failed this month** — failed payments ÷ every payment billed this month.
- **Collections (90d)** — collected ÷ billed across the current and previous two
  billing months. Collected means `paidDate` is set; late payers that eventually
  settled still count. This is the "are we actually getting paid?" number.

### Overdue payments

A server-paged table of every `late` or `failed` payment, 25 rows per page,
newest billing month first and largest amount first within a month. Columns are
household (flat and resident), society, billing month, amount, days late,
status, and action. Days late is computed from the first of the billing month,
matching the seed's due-date convention.

This table has pagination but no filters: the query is already scoped to the
only rows an operator can act on. Marking one paid is a single click.

## The data behind it

Payments are generated per active subscription-month, from `startDate` to
`endDate` or today. The outcome mix is deliberate:

- **Normal months:** 88% paid, 8% late, 4% failed.
- **Current month:** 58% paid, 5% late, 4% failed, 33% pending.
- **Risk-cohort months:** 20% paid, 50% late, 30% failed. The bad window starts
  one month before the household's complaint cluster, so the revenue story and
  the churn story line up.

Plan prices are ₹499 for starter, ₹799 for standard, and ₹1,199 for premium. The
seed creates roughly 1,150 households and about 25,000 payments over 24 months.
About 13% of households enter the risk cohort and roughly 60% of those cancel
within one to three months of the bad window.

MRR, ARPU, and plan mix all come from subscription spans rather than stored
monthly totals, so "who was active when" has one definition across the route.

## Interactions

- **Mark paid.** The clicked row's button enters a pending state, pinned to that
  payment id so other rows stay idle. On success a toast names the resident,
  flat, billing month, and amount. `onSettled` invalidates `getKpis`,
  `getPaymentStatusByMonth`, and `getOverdue`, so the KPI row, outcomes chart,
  and table move together.
- **Failure.** There is no optimistic write. If the mutation fails, the row is
  unchanged in the database and the toast reports the error; the invalidation
  still runs.
- **Page.** Pagination is server-side: the page number is part of the tRPC input
  and the query key. A deferred copy of the state drives the rows, so the
  current page dims while the next one loads instead of flashing a skeleton.
- **No optimistic payment.** Showing a recovered payment before the write lands
  would overstate collections, so the table waits for the server.

## Trade-offs & next

- MRR is a point-in-time reconstruction. It ignores upgrades or downgrades
  inside a month, and the seed gives each subscription one plan for its life.
- Collections is a three-billing-month window, not a ledger reconciliation. A
  late payment counts as collected in the month it was billed, not the month the
  cash arrived.
- There is no dunning workflow, retry scheduling, or promise-to-pay date.
- No revenue forecast: the history is too short to make one honest.
- No per-area revenue view, and the overdue table has no search or filters.
