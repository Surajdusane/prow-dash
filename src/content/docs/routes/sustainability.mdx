## Why this route exists

A piped RO subscription has a credible environmental story: water delivered
through a dispenser displaces single-use 20 L plastic jars. But the claim is
only useful if it is **defensible**. A vague "we saved the planet" number is
worse than no page at all.

The page answers **how much water did we serve, and how many jars did that
displace?** It also shows how to present an estimate honestly: the formula, plan
assumptions, and exclusions sit next to the number.

## What I chose - and what I left out

| Option                     | Why I rejected it                      | What I chose instead                |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| A single "jars saved" hero | No audit trail                         | Tiles, formula, and a method note   |
| Real-time IoT litres       | The data is simulated; would overclaim | Subscription-days × plan litres/day |
| A map of impact            | Does not add to the decision           | Cumulative trends and a by-plan bar |
| Carbon or energy claims    | Not modeled; would be invented         | Water and jars only                 |
| A forecast                 | Too uncertain                          | Historical estimate only            |

## What's on screen

Live route: `/sustainability`. The page is `force-dynamic`;
`batchPrefetchSustainability()` warms the totals, the 24-month trend, and the
by-plan breakdown in one batch. Each section has its own `Suspense` boundary and
a matching skeleton. There are no filters and no mutations: this is a reporting
surface, and 24 months is the right default at this data volume.

### Impact tiles

Four tiles from `getImpactTotals`: litres served, 20 L jars avoided, households
served, and months live.

These are **estimates**, not meter readings. Litres are active subscription-days
× the plan's daily estimate; jars are litres ÷ 20. Households served counts
every distinct household that has ever subscribed, including cancelled ones.
Months live runs from the earliest start to today, divided by 30.44 days and
rounded, with a floor of one.

### Cumulative litres served

**Visual:** an area chart of cumulative litres by month over 24 months. **Why:**
cumulative impact is a stock, not a flow; an area reads as build-up over time,
while monthly bars would read as a workload.

The query fetches every subscription span once. For each month it takes the
overlap of each span with that month,
`[max(spanStart, monthStart), min(spanEnd, nextMonthStart))`, multiplies active
days by the plan's litres/day, and adds the result into a running total. The
current month is partial: an open span ends at today.

### Cumulative jars avoided

**Visual:** an area chart of cumulative 20 L jars avoided - the same story in
the unit a customer or investor can picture. **Why:** it is deliberately
parallel to the litres chart, so a reader can switch units without re-learning

### Impact by plan

**Visual:** a vertical bar of all-time litres per plan, each bar labelled with
its jar equivalent. **Why:** there are three categories, and the plan drives
usage - 8, 12, or 18 L per day. A bar keeps the ranking readable; a pie would
hide how different the volumes are.

A custom ECharts series gives every bar its plan colour and a top label. The
tooltip repeats both units. Plan order is fixed - starter, standard, premium -
so the chart does not reshuffle.

### Method note

**Visual:** a card with the formula and assumptions. **Why:** the number is only
credible if the method is visible; a footnote would be treated as fine print.

The card is a server component and imports `PLAN_LITRES_PER_DAY` and
`JAR_SIZE_LITRES` from `src/db/queries/plans.ts`, so the rates and jar size in
the copy share one source with the query. It defines active days and names the
exclusions: dispenser flushing, reject water, and delivery losses.

## The data behind it

- **Subscription spans are the source of truth.** A span starts on the join date
  and ends on the cancel date, or stays open. The seed creates one subscription
  per household, so revenue, retention, and impact read the same calendar.
- **Active days are end-exclusive.** The cancel date is not an active day:
  `activeDays = daysBetween(start, endDate ?? today)`, clamped at zero.
- **Rates are constants, not measurements.** Starter 8 L/day, standard 12,
  premium 18. The query constants mirror the seed reference, and the method note
  imports the query constants.
- **Jars are a unit conversion.** One jar is 20 L. No jar count is stored, so a
  change to the jar size changes every chart consistently.
- **The trend covers 24 months**, with the current month incomplete because open
  spans end at today.
- **Scope is conservative.** Flushing, reject water, and delivery losses are
  excluded, so this is not a lifecycle analysis.

## Interactions

- **Read-only.** No mutations, filters, or drill-downs. The route reports; it
  does not change the business.
- **One window.** The trend always shows 24 months. A date filter would add
  state without changing the answer at this volume.
- **Theme-aware charts.** Both trends and the by-plan bars use the shared chart
  palette; the by-plan card also adapts its label colour to the theme.
- **Server-rendered.** All three queries are prefetched and hydrated into
  suspense hooks; there is no client-side fetch waterfall.

## What to notice as a reviewer

1. Read the method note next to `src/db/queries/plans.ts`. The rates in the card
   are imported, so the copy and the query share one constant.
2. Compare the last point of the litres chart with the last point of the jars
   chart. The ratio should be exactly 20; the jars series is computed from
   litres in the same query.
3. Compare the by-plan bars with the daily rates. Premium uses 18 L/day against
   starter's 8, but height also depends on accumulated subscription-days, so the
   tallest bar is not automatically the highest rate.

## Trade-offs & next

- Litres/day is a plan constant, not measured consumption. A household that
  drinks less than its plan still counts at the full rate.
- Jars avoided assumes one 20 L jar per 20 L served. It does not model refill
  returns, jar reuse, or the plastic used to make a jar.
- Reject water, flushing, and delivery losses are excluded. The result is
  conservative as a displacement claim, but not a lifecycle analysis.
- No carbon or energy claims, because neither is modeled.
- No per-society or per-area view, and no third-party verification.
- The seed gives each subscription one plan for its life, so the by-plan split
  does not handle mid-life upgrades.
- The 24-month window is fixed; there is no date range, export, or forecast.
