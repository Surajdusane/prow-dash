## 60-second summary

- Churn is usually visible before it happens: complaints cluster, payments go
  late, service misses stack up.
- I built a transparent 0–100 risk score from four signal groups: complaints,
  payments, SLA misses, and tenure.
- The score is computed by one shared function used by the seed and by the
  "Recompute risk" mutation.
- The `/customers` page turns the score into a sortable, filterable action list
  with a household drawer.
- The seed injects a known risk cohort, so the score can be checked against
  outcomes instead of being decorative.

## Why this exists

A cancellation is the end of a longer story: an open complaint, a failed
payment, a missed SLA. The signals are there; nobody has a ranked list of who to
call next.

- Reactive retention is expensive: a cancellation is already lost revenue.
- Support needs a ranked list, not another dashboard of charts.
- The reason behind the risk must be explainable to a support agent on the
  phone.
- The score should be cheap to recompute and consistent everywhere.

This is a triage tool: who should support call this week, and why?

## Options I considered

| Option                                       | Why it looks attractive                                 | Why I did not choose it                                        |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Supervised classifier                        | Learns non-linear patterns; improves with labelled data | No labelled production data; hard to explain; needs monitoring |
| Simple filters (for example, complaints > 2) | Trivial to implement                                    | Ignores payment and SLA signals; no ranking                    |
| Rules-based 0–100 score                      | Explainable, cheap, easy to validate                    | Weights are hand-tuned; not calibrated probabilities           |

**I chose the rules-based score.** It uses all four signal groups, produces a
ranking instead of a yes/no flag, and shows a support agent which inputs moved a
household up the list. The weights are opinions until they are calibrated
against real outcomes.

## What I chose

An additive 0–100 score with three bands, used as a ranking tool, not a
probability. The bands create an operating rhythm:

- **low** (`< 34`) — monitor.
- **medium** (`34–66`) — review.
- **high** (`≥ 67`) — call.

`computeHouseholdRisk` runs in the seed, the recompute mutation, and any future
cron job, so there is no second implementation to drift.

## How the score works

```text
complaintScore = min(45, complaints90d × 9
                        + highSeverity90d × 5
                        + unresolved90d × 5)
paymentScore   = min(45, latePayments6mo × 7
                        + failedPayments6mo × 11)
slaScore       = min(16, slaMissedTickets12mo × 4)
tenureScore    = 8 if tenure < 90d
                 5 if tenure < 180d
                 2 if tenure < 365d
                 else 0

risk = clamp(0, 100, round(complaintScore + paymentScore
                           + slaScore + tenureScore))
```

Why each group is weighted:

- **Complaints are the strongest explicit signal.** Nine points per complaint in
  90 days, plus five for high severity and five for unresolved. An open,
  high-severity complaint is a support failure, not just a data point.
- **Payment behaviour is the strongest implicit signal.** A late payment is
  seven points; a failed payment is eleven; combined cap 45.
- **SLA misses capture frustration that may never become a complaint.** Four
  points per missed ticket in 12 months, capped at 16. A household can be
  unhappy without calling.
- **Newer households have less history.** Tenure adds 8, 5, 2, or 0 points as
  the relationship matures. It is a small uncertainty adjustment, not a churn
  signal on its own.

The additive maximum is 114 before clamping, so multiple strong signals can
saturate the score at 100. That also means scores at 100 cannot be ordered by
severity.

Full function: <SourceLink path="src/db/queries/risk.ts" label="risk.ts" />.
`recomputeAllRiskScores` is the only writer of `households.risk_score`: it
aggregates the three signals in SQL, computes scores in TS, and updates in
chunks of 400 with one `UPDATE ... FROM (VALUES ...)`, skipping unchanged rows.

Adding or resolving a complaint refreshes the drawer; neither auto-rescores.
That is deliberate: a support lead can make several edits, then recompute once.
The score changes on that click.

## Validation

The seed injects a **~13% risk cohort** with a deliberately bad recent window:

- 2–4 complaints inside the window, skewed toward its end so the signal stays
  inside the 90-day scoring window.
- Late and failed payments that start slightly before the complaint cluster.
  Inside the window the payment mix is roughly 50% late and 30% failed, versus
  8% late and 4% failed outside it.
- About 60% of the cohort cancels 30–90 days after the window, when the timeline
  allows.

The score is recomputed at the end of the seed, so the demo has a score and an
outcome for the same household. But this page publishes no precision or recall
number. The cohort flag is generated in memory and not stored on `households`,
so a live query cannot join scores to cohort membership. The seed is also one
deterministic fixture, not a validation set; a number from it would describe the
generator, not the business.

### How I would validate this

| Metric                     | Definition                                      | What it tells you                         |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Precision at the high band | Cohort members in high ÷ all households in high | How many high-risk calls are real         |
| Recall at the high band    | Cohort members in high ÷ all cohort members     | How many at-risk households are captured  |
| Lift at the top decile     | Churn rate in the top 10% ÷ base churn rate     | Whether the ranking beats random outreach |
| Band calibration           | Observed churn rate for low, medium, high       | Whether 34 and 67 mean anything           |

Use a time-based split: score each household at time `T` using only data before
`T`, then observe cancellations in `(T, T+90]`. Persist the cohort label, or
reconstruct it from the window, so precision and recall can be joined. Tune the
band cutoffs against support capacity: a team that can call 50 households a week
should pick the cutoff that fills 50 slots with the best precision, not the
cutoff that maximises overall accuracy.

The demo shows the mechanism, not a validated lift.

## The data behind it

| Signal                   | Table                  | Window    | Weight          |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- | --------- | --------------- |
| Complaints               | `complaints`           | 90 days   | 45 max          |
| High-severity complaints | `complaints`           | 90 days   | included above  |
| Unresolved complaints    | `complaints`           | 90 days   | included above  |
| Late payments            | `payments`             | 6 months  | 45 max combined |
| Failed payments          | `payments`             | 6 months  | included above  |
| SLA misses               | `service_tickets`      | 12 months | 16 max          |
| Tenure                   | `households.join_date` | current   | 8 max           |

The payment window is the current month plus the previous five. The SLA window
is the last 365 days. The score uses complaint `severity` and `resolved`,
payment `status`, ticket `slaMet`, and `join_date`; it ignores category and
amount.

| Surface           | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Fed by                |

| Risk table        | Server-paged action list, default sorted by risk. Filters: risk band, area, status. Columns: household, society/area, plan, tenure, risk score, complaints 90d, late + failed 6mo, last complaint, status. Clicking a row opens the drawer. | `getRiskTable`        |
| Risk distribution | Donut of the active book split by low, medium, and high, with counts and shares.                                                                                                                                                            | `getRiskDistribution` |
| Complaint trend   | Multi-line chart of monthly complaints by category over 12 months.                                                                                                                                                                          | `getComplaintTrend`   |
| Household drawer  | Risk ring; subscription; dispenser; 30-day TDS and purity sparkline; latest 5 tickets; latest 6 complaints; latest 6 payments; add complaint.                                                                                               | `getById`             |
| Recompute risk    | One-click, confirmed mutation that rescores the whole book. Invalidates the table, distribution, KPIs, and drawer.                                                                                                                          | `recomputeRisk`       |

The drawer is the "why": the ring gives the number, the sections give the
evidence. The donut and trend show whether risk is concentrated and which
category is driving it.

Why these visuals:

- A table for triage, because support acts row by row.
- A donut for part-to-whole with three bands, because the question is "how much
  of the book is high risk?"
- A multi-line chart for category trends, because a single total hides whether
  the problem is water quality, service delay, billing, or device malfunction.
- A sparkline for compact telemetry inside a drawer, and a ring for one score
  with band context.

## What to watch as a reviewer

1. Open [/customers](/customers), set the risk-band filter to **High**, and open
   the first household. The drawer should show the evidence behind the score:
   recent complaints, late or failed payments, SLA-missed tickets, and the
   30-day TDS/purity curve.
2. Open `src/db/queries/risk.ts`. `computeHouseholdRisk` is the entire scoring
   model; `recomputeAllRiskScores` is the only writer of
   `households.risk_score`. Check the 90-day, 6-month, and 12-month windows
   against the table above.
3. Add a complaint to a household, resolve it, then click **Recompute risk**.
   The complaint history updates immediately; the score changes on recompute,
   and the table re-sorts. That separation is intentional.

## Trade-offs and limitations

- Hand-tuned weights, not calibrated probabilities.
- A ranking, not a probability of churn.
- The seed cohort is synthetic; real validation needs real outcomes.
- Fixed windows (90 days, 6 months, 12 months) may not fit every business.
- No causal claim: complaints correlate with churn, but the demo does not prove
  causation.
- No per-plan or per-area calibration.
- Scores saturate at 100 because the additive maximum is 114, so the highest
  band loses ordering.
- Support-logged complaints are a biased sample; unhappy households that never
  call are invisible.

## What I would do next

- Calibrate the weights against real cancellation data with a time-based
  backtest.
- Add precision/recall monitoring and a review queue for borderline scores.
- Separate "risk of churn" from "risk of dissatisfaction."
- Add suggested actions (call, discount, free filter change) based on the
  dominant signal.
- Add a risk-score time series per household.
